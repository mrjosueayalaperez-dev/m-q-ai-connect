MOOSE — RESEARCH ASSIGNMENT 2

M&Q UNIVERSITY + MNQ SCALP STACKS COMMAND CENTER AI INTEGRATION

Mission

Research how we can responsibly add more useful AI capabilities to both M&Q University and the MNQ Scalp Stacks Command Center.

The founder's direction:

- University = intelligent teacher.
- Command Center = intelligent analyst.
- M&Q AI CONNECT = permission-controlled intelligence layer.
- Applications remain independent.
- Private student data never crosses into trading systems.
- Trading data never crosses into the University.

This assignment is about technology, architecture, capabilities, costs, and implementation readiness.

It is NOT about Mozilla grants, legal eligibility, LLC registration, or fiscal sponsorship.

PART A — M&Q UNIVERSITY

Audit the actual existing University codebase.

Investigate:

1. AI Professor grounded in the existing lessons.
2. Bilingual English/Spanish AI teaching.
3. Semantic lesson search.
4. Intelligent quizzes with verified answer keys.
5. Personalized explanations based on student progress.
6. Voice interaction and accessibility.
7. Low-cost models and alternative AI providers.

Preserve the structured curriculum and existing educational standards.

Determine which capabilities can be added without rebuilding the University.

PART B — MNQ SCALP STACKS COMMAND CENTER

Audit the actual dashboard and backend.

Investigate:

1. AI trading analyst.
2. Strategy A and Strategy B explanations.
3. Signal validation explanations.
4. Risk-monitoring explanations.
5. PAPER trading journal summaries.
6. Deterministic reconciliation and discrepancy explanations.
7. Daily intelligence reports.
8. Bilingual operator assistance.

The AI must not execute trades, change risk settings, bypass safety controls, or independently authorize actions.

All trading calculations and risk decisions remain deterministic.

PART C — M&Q AI CONNECT

Investigate how both applications can use a common intelligence capability without merging their databases.

Evaluate:

- Provider/model access.
- Permission controls.
- Capability registry.
- Shared educational knowledge assets.
- Independent project identities.
- Privacy boundaries.
- Auditability.
- Model replacement and portability.

No central system should collect private student and trading records.

PART D — TECHNOLOGY AND COST COMPARISON

Compare realistic options from:

- OpenAI
- Anthropic
- Google
- Vercel AI Gateway
- Supabase
- Other credible low-cost alternatives

For each, report:

- Capabilities.
- Actual pricing.
- Free-tier limits.
- Privacy and retention policies.
- Vendor lock-in.
- Integration complexity.
- Best use case.

Use current official pricing and documentation. Separate verified prices from estimates.

PART E — LOWEST-COST PROTOTYPES

Identify the three best prototypes:

1. One for the University.
2. One for the Command Center.
3. One for the shared intelligence architecture.

For each, provide scope, dependencies, estimated cost, acceptance tests, and risks.

REQUIRED VERIFICATION

Use the actual repositories and deployed code as they stand.

Do not assume an implementation is tested because code exists.

Label findings:

- VERIFIED
- IMPLEMENTED
- TESTED
- PROPOSED
- UNKNOWN

Reconcile earlier dashboard safety findings, especially persistence, risk controls, and journal integrity.

FINAL DELIVERABLE

Produce one report covering:

A. Current architecture inventory.

B. University AI opportunities.

C. Dashboard AI opportunities.

D. Shared CONNECT architecture.

E. Three lowest-cost prototypes.

F. Technology and cost comparison.

G. Risks and acceptance criteria.

H. Recommended implementation order.

RESTRICTIONS

Research and read-only auditing only.

Do not:

- Modify code.
- Create branches or commits.
- Purchase services.
- Create API keys.
- Enable AI providers.
- Merge or deploy.
- Change privacy settings.
